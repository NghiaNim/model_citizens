The 2026 United States Senate election in Kansas will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect a member of the United States Senate to represent the state of Kansas. Republican incumbent Roger Marshall is seeking a second term. He is being challenged by Democratic pastor Adam Hamilton.

In the August 4 primary elections, Marshall won the Republican nomination against minimal opposition with 79.9% of the vote. Hamilton, the senior pastor and founder of the United Methodist Church of the Resurrection, won the Democratic nomination with 34.6% of the vote among a field of eight candidates.

Democrats have not won a Senate election in Kansas since 1932.

Background

[edit]

Though generally considered to be a red state,[1][2] Kansas has shown greater competitiveness over time due to shifts in the traditionally Republican-leaning Kansas City suburbs.[3] While Republicans control both houses of the Kansas Legislature,[4] Democrats control the governorship of Kansas, which they flipped from Republican control in 2018, and held in 2022.[2] Kansas has not elected a Democrat to the U.S. Senate since 1932.[2] Incumbent senator Roger Marshall was elected in 2020 with 53.2% of the vote, winning by 11.4% despite expectations of a close race.[5][6]

General election

[edit]

An issue raised during the campaign was Roger Marshall's history of suing over 700 of his patients for unpaid bills while he worked as an OB/GYN in Kansas. The New York Times reported that in at least 81 cases, patients were arrested at the request of Marshall's lawyers for failure to appear in court for their unpaid debts. There were 13 additional cases where an arrest warrant was requested, but the outcome is unknown. The New York Times additionally interviewed several of the patients who were sued by Marshall, most of whom said they lived paycheck-to-paycheck and did not have the money to pay the bills.[33] Hamilton produced an advertisement featuring a woman who said she was arrested for missing a $50 payment to Marshall. The Marshall campaign sent a letter alleging the ad was "defamatory" because the woman was arrested for failing to appear in court over larger debts to him, rather than for missing one bill, and that she was subject to several other court proceedings.[34][35][36]

The election received national attention for the use of an antisemitic slur by President Donald Trump during a Marshall campaign rally in order to deflect allegations about the issue.[37][38][39][40] Former Republican representative Barbara Comstock said that "Trump once again lets loose his anti-Semitic rhetoric" in the incident.[41] The White House did not respond to a request for comment about the President's use of "shylock".[41] It was not the first time that Trump had used the term in the campaign trail, and he had also received criticism for its use in 2025, when he had claimed that he was unaware that the term was an antisemitic slur against Jews.[41] A statement denouncing the incident, though not denouncing Trump by name was released by the Anti-Defamation League.[42][43]

The Kansas City Star described the Marshall and Hamilton campaigns as making several exaggerated or false claims regarding Jeffrey Epstein, the Epstein files, and a case of sexual abuse at Church of the Resurrection.[44]

September 12

[edit]

The first debate was informal, and held at the Kansas State Fair.[47]

It was marked by a "raucous" and "vocal" crowd "that was far more loudly opposed to the incumbent U.S. senator."[47]

October 21

[edit]

FOX4 hosted the October debate. Economic issues, including gas prices, inflation, tariffs, and the American affordability crisis were the leading topics of the debate.[48] Marshall called for maintaining high taxes on imports and other tariffs, while Hamilton argued that they were a form of taxation that "functioned as an unpredictable tax on Kansans".[48]

Foreign policy was also a central issue in the debate. Marshall supported continued operations in the Iran War, while Hamilton called for an exit as soon as it was sufficiently possible.[48] Marshall defended Trump's agreement to get diesel from and his attempts to geopolitically reapproach Russia, while Hamilton claimed it would be "selling out our allies, the Ukrainians" in the current Russo-Ukrainian war.[48] Marshall referred to Israel as "one of America's greatest allies".[48]

Marshall expressed support for a "light touch" on AI regulation and said that America must achieve superintelligence in the arms race before China.[48] Hamilton argued that progress in artificial intelligence poses an "potential existential threat to the human race" and that "we can't just go with a light touch and hope everybody does their very best".[48]
